FAQ

The questions every buyer asks.

Wait — isn't a monthly fee exactly the renting you rail against?
Fair question, and here's the honest answer: you're renting the watching — the monitoring, the classification, my eyes on every brief. That's genuinely ongoing work, so it's honestly priced as ongoing. But the record — every signal, score, profile, and brief the system produces — is yours from day one, exports whenever you ask, and leaves with you if you cancel. A retainer you can quit anytime, that hands you everything it built on the way out, isn't a trap. It's a service.
How is this different from Klue or Crayon?
Those are excellent platforms for organizations with a thousand reps and a dedicated CI owner to feed them. They start around $15–40K a year — before the salary of the person required to run them. This is the inverse: the analyst is included, the price is flat and public, and it's scoped to a mid-market team that needs to know, not to administrate a platform.
How is this different from the $20–80/month alert tools?
Those tools answer "did something happen?" This answers "does it matter, and what should we do?" Every signal is scored against your positioning — defined with you at onboarding, not guessed by a generic model — and every brief is read by a human before it reaches your team. The self-serve tools also can't answer questions: ask them "has X raised prices twice this year?" and you get a search box. Ask the desk, and you get an answer with dates.
What's actually in a brief?
Three parts, every time: what moved (the signals that cleared the noise floor, scored), what it means for you (read through your Positioning Profile — not generic commentary), and what I'd do about it (2–3 recommended actions, concrete enough to forward to a rep). If nothing meaningful happened, the brief says so in one paragraph — I will never pad a quiet fortnight into fake urgency.
Where does the data come from?
Public sources only. Competitors' public social accounts, public pricing and product pages, and the marketing emails they send to anyone who signs up. No logged-in scraping, no gray-area access, no pretexting. If it's not something you could see yourself with a browser and an inbox, it's not in the system.
Who reads this stuff before it hits my Slack?
Me. Every brief is drafted by the system and reviewed, edited, or rewritten by me before it sends. That's the difference between this and a feed — and it's why the price is what it is. You're not paying for compute. You're paying for the judgment call on what a 4/5 signal actually is in your market.
Can I get both systems?
Yes — and they compound. The Watchtower tells you a competitor just raised prices on your best segment; the Outbound Engine puts that exact angle in front of their customers the same week. Most clients start with one. The ones who add the second usually do it within a quarter.
